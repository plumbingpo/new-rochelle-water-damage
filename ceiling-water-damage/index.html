Ceiling Water Damage Repair in New Rochelle

A brown ring on the ceiling is the last thing to show up, not the first. Before any mark shows, water has traveled down a joist, saturated the insulation and collected on top of the drywall. Common sources are the roof, an air conditioning drain, a pipe running overhead, or the bathroom one story up. We trace it to the source before anything gets patched.

A coat of paint over the spot solves nothing. Moisture left in the cavity brings the discoloration back and lets mold get going on the paper side in 24 to 48 hours. Our crew removes no more ceiling than necessary, takes out soaked insulation, dries the framing and the drywall that stays, and finishes with a patch, texture and paint that match. If the ceiling has started to droop, treat it differently. Stay out from under it and call.

Warning Signs

  • Seams, tape lines and fastener heads becoming visible across the ceiling
  • Soft, bowed or sagging spots you can feel with a gentle push
  • Stale, mildew like odor you cannot trace to anything you can see
  • Water dripping from a light fixture, vent or smoke detector
  • Blistered, bubbling or flaking paint on the ceiling
  • Dark spots or speckled growth forming on the ceiling surface
What We Do

Our Ceiling Water Damage Repair Process

1

Find the Source

Using thermal imaging and moisture meters, our team follows the water to its origin and inspects the attic or room overhead. Repairs are pointless until that leak is shut down.

2

Make It Safe

We cut power to any wet fixture and move or cover the furniture underneath. Where water is trapped overhead, it gets drained under control into containers.

3

Remove and Dry Out

Wet insulation is removed along with any drywall that cannot stay. Drying equipment, meaning a dehumidifier and air movers, works on the framing and adjacent drywall, with antimicrobial treatment for water that was not clean.

4

Repair and Finish

After the meter shows normal levels, new insulation and drywall go up, joints are taped and finished, the texture is matched and a stain blocking primer goes on ahead of paint.

What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

Mold Above You

Even when the ceiling feels dry to the touch, the batts and the unseen face of the drywall remain damp, giving mold a hidden place to spread.

Water Travels Sideways

The wet spot is often not below the leak, since water rides pipes, ducts and joists before it falls. Cutting in just under the mark may leave damp areas elsewhere untouched.

Wet Lights and Wiring

The damp area usually includes fan mounts, recessed fixtures and wiring boxes. A short is possible when water enters a light, which is why the breaker should be shut off.

A Ceiling That Lets Go

Wet drywall is far heavier than dry and loses its grip on the fasteners. An entire piece can fall from a bulging spot onto anything below.

If Nothing Is Done

The Damage Timeline

The first few hours

Water Spreads Above

Moisture follows the joists and the insulation drinks it in. From inside the room everything can appear normal, since the drywall is taking on water on the hidden face.

Hours 24 to 48

Stains and Bubbles

A stain forms and paint starts to blister. Soaked insulation weighs on the drywall while mold can start on its paper face.

Day 3 to 7

Sagging Sets In

Drywall softens and pulls away from its screws. The ceiling bows between joists, seams open up and a musty odor reaches the room.

Past one week

Risk of Collapse

Waterlogged sections can fall, bringing wet insulation with them. The repair zone is far bigger at this stage, with mold well rooted inside the cavity.
