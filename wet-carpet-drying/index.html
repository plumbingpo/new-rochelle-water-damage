Wet Carpet Drying in New Rochelle

A wet carpet is hiding a lot more water than you can see. Up top the fibers may seem barely damp, yet the pad has filled up the way a sponge does and the subfloor under it has gotten wet as well. Towels and a shop vacuum reach the top layer and little else. For New Rochelle homes, our crew handles the extraction, meters the carpet, pad and subfloor, and runs drying gear that gets to the bottom layer.

Whether the carpet can be saved depends on two things, how clean the water was and how long it has been wet. If the water came out of a clean supply line and we reach it fast, the carpet can normally stay down while it dries. Water from an appliance counts as gray, so the pad is frequently removed while the carpet is cleaned and treated. Both layers, carpet and pad, are removed when the water was sewage or came in from outside flooding.

What It Looks Like

  • Dampness that spreads outward on its own, well after the leak was stopped
  • A floor that squeaks or feels soft under the carpet where it got wet
  • Loose, wavy sections where the tack strip no longer holds the carpet
  • Dark, rusty lines along the wall where the tack strip has gotten wet
  • The room smells sour and stale, especially after it has been closed for a while
  • A squishy feel under your feet, or wet socks after walking across the room
What We Do

What Happens After You Call

1

Map the Wet Area

Our first job is finding the outer limit of the water, under walls and in closets too, using a thermal camera and moisture meters. Then we determine which category of water it is, as that tells us what is safe to keep.

2

Pull Out the Water

With weighted extractors, carpet and the pad beneath it are pressed and emptied in one pass. This removes far more than a wand alone, and it shortens the drying time that follows.

3

Dry All the Layers

Depending on the job, we float the carpet on moving air or lift it and remove the pad. Our air movers and dehumidifiers keep going until meters show a dry carpet and a dry subfloor.

4

Clean and Reset

Where needed, an antimicrobial goes on the subfloor and carpet, then new pad is laid if the old was removed, and the carpet is stretched back on its tack strip and cleaned.

The Damage Timeline

The first few hours

Water Moves Outward

The pad wicks water well past the visible wet spot. The tack strip starts taking on water as it creeps below the walls toward the room next door.

24 to 48 hours

Smell and Mold Start

The damp pad breeds bacteria, and the sour smell of wet carpet follows. Mold can begin growing on the backing and on dust trapped deep in the fibers.

Day 3 to 7

The Carpet Breaks Down

As the latex bonding the backing gives out, the carpet delaminates and starts to ripple. Rust forms on the tack strip nails while the subfloor underneath swells.

After a week

Past Saving

Carpet and pad left wet this long generally cannot stay. Mold has often reached the subfloor and the bottom of the drywall, so the job grows.

Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

Water Quality Drops

Even clean water goes bad the longer it sits. It collects bacteria and dirt from the pad and fibers, which can turn a simple drying job into a removal.

Mold on the Backing

The dark, damp underside of the carpet is where mold usually begins. Your nose finds it well before anything shows on the surface.

Damp Base of the Walls

The base of each wall is where water moving beneath the carpet ends up. Drywall and baseboards wick it upward, and nobody notices because the carpet covers the edge.

Swollen Subfloor

Subfloors made of OSB or particleboard puff up and fall apart if they remain wet. Squeaks, soft spots and raised seams show up weeks after the spill is forgotten.
