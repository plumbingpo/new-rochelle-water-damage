Dealing With Appliance Leak Water Damage in New Rochelle

Nearly every appliance leak we handle comes from one of three sources. Washer supply hoses can burst and keep spraying at full pressure until the valve is closed. Dishwashers usually leak a little at a time, at the drain hose or door seal, hidden beneath the cabinets. The ice maker line on a refrigerator is a thin tube, and it can drip for months behind the unit. No two of these call for the same cleanup.

Our New Rochelle crew starts by pulling the appliance out and finding where the water went. Kitchen leaks tend to end up beneath the cabinets and underneath the floor. With a laundry leak we look at the rooms next to it and the wall behind the washer, plus the ceiling below when the laundry sits on an upper floor. We extract what is standing, measure what has soaked in, and set drying equipment sized to the wet area.

Before We Arrive

  1. Turn Off the ApplianceIf the outlet and the floor next to it are both dry, end the cycle and take the plug out of the wall. If not, switch it off at the breaker instead.
  2. Close the Supply ValveFor a washing machine, close both the cold and hot valves on the wall at its back. The dishwasher valve is under the kitchen sink. A refrigerator line has a small valve below or behind the unit.
  3. Soak Up Standing WaterGetting reachable water up with a mop or towels stops it from moving under the cabinets and the floor. Do not vacuum unless the machine is rated for wet and dry use.
  4. Keep the Door ClosedA dishwasher or washer that still holds water should stay closed. When the door opens mid-cycle, the full load of water can pour out on the floor.
  5. Photograph the Failed PartPhotograph the failed fitting, valve or hose, and hold on to the part itself. Your insurer and the appliance maker may both ask to see it.
How We Fix It

Our Appliance Leak Water Damage Process

After making sure the supply is shut and moving the disconnected appliance, our crew follows the water with meters and a thermal camera into nearby walls, under flooring and beneath cabinets.

Standing water is extracted from floors and carpet. To reach water caught below cabinets or under floating floors, our crew pulls toe kicks or takes up boards.

Why It Happens

What Causes It

  • Standpipes that clog, or the drain hose from a washer coming loose from one.
  • Poor installation, such as a dishwasher set out of level or a supply fitting not fully tightened.
  • Too much detergent or an overloaded drum that makes the machine overflow.
  • Aging rubber supply hoses on a washing machine, which weaken and then split under pressure.
  • A dishwasher with a failed pump seal, a cracked drain hose or a worn door gasket.
Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

Wall Bases and Trim

Drywall edges and baseboards pull up water that stands on a kitchen or laundry room floor. Since it is out of view, the wall behind the machine tends to be wetter than the rest.

Downstairs Ceiling Damage

The ceiling under an upper floor laundry catches whatever water makes it past the floor above. Drywall there sags and stains while the insulation over it holds water.

Kitchen Cabinets

Dishwasher leaks soak cabinet sides and toe kicks from underneath. Panels swell and come apart, and a wet cabinet box can keep the wall behind it damp for weeks.

Hidden Mold at the Back

Hardly anyone looks at the dark, warm space behind a dishwasher or refrigerator. A slow drip there feeds mold on the wall and floor long before anyone notices.

Will Insurance Pay?

Insurance and Appliance Leak Water Damage

A hose that bursts or a machine that overflows without warning is the kind of sudden and accidental event homeowners policies often cover. What is typically paid is the water damage, while the failed part and the appliance are left out. Slow seepage from a dishwasher or ice maker line is a harder claim, because gradual leaks usually are not covered. Certain insurers will want to know the hose's age and whether it was kept up. Hold on to the part that failed, photograph everything ahead of cleanup, and review what your policy says.

What It Costs

What Affects the Cost

  • Whether the water was clean supply water or gray discharge that needs sanitizing.
  • Duration of the leak, particularly a hidden drip that went unnoticed behind the machine.
  • Whether cabinets and flooring can be dried in place or need to be removed.
  • Presence of mold, which adds removal work in front of the drying.
